Economic performance score: methodology

Alfredo Ghana Economic Model. Version 2.1, 5 November 2026.

What it measures

The score is a single figure from 0 to 100 showing how Ghana's economy stands against its own record since 2007. A score of 50 is a typical year, and 100 would match Ghana's best year on every measure. It is not a percentage of Ghana's potential, and it is not an official statistic.

The calculation

  1. Each of 33 measures is compared with Ghana's yearly results since 2007. That is the first year growth data exists for all four sectors.
  2. A measure scores the share of those years it beats, with ties counted as half. For inflation, debt, bad loans, unemployment and a falling cedi, lower scores higher.
  3. Measures are averaged within nine parts. Within sector output, each sector's weight is its share of GDP times its linkage to other sectors. Linkage is the average of its backward and forward Leontief multipliers.
  4. The nine parts are averaged with equal weight.

The measures

Adding the last two parts moved the score from 53 to 51. Replacing out-of-date 2024 budget figures with the IMF's 2025 results (budget balance −1.3% and revenue 15.7% of GDP, IMF Country Report 26/212) then moved it back to 53. The IMF's 2025 balance is on a commitment basis, while earlier years use its World Economic Outlook definition, so the two are close but not identical. Child deaths, safe water, enrolment, yields and food production rise or fall steadily over decades, so they are scored on their yearly change. Scored as plain levels they would sit near their best almost every year, which would have added about eight points. Oil series start in 2011, when production began. The 2026 oil figures are for January to June, with receipts doubled to make a full year. Gold output, cocoa output and tourism are shown but not scored, because a complete comparable record back to 2007 was not available.

Sources and timing

Most yearly data comes from the World Bank's World Development Indicators. Budget balance and revenue come from the IMF World Economic Outlook via DBnomics. Debt comes from the IMF to 2019, then the Ministry of Finance and Bank of Ghana. Inflation, the cedi, debt, the current account and bad loans use the latest readings from Ghana Statistical Service and Bank of Ghana. These recent readings are compared with yearly history, and the cedi's latest change is measured over about 12 months.

Testing the method

Five alternatives on the same data gave 51 to 53 when the main method gave 53 in November 2026: equal weight for every measure, GDP share without linkage, equal sector weights, ranking from 2012, and scoring between the best and worst years.

In a backtest ranking each year only against earlier years, the score was 44 in 2014, before the 2015 IMF programme. It was 55 in 2019 before the COVID shock, and 54 in 2021 before the 2022 default, when it fell to 38. It records trouble as it arrives, but it is not a reliable early warning. Three events are too few to prove much, and the method was designed after they happened.

Comparison with other countries

A separate ranking compares Ghana with Côte d'Ivoire, Kenya, Nigeria, Rwanda, Senegal, South Africa, Tanzania and Uganda. Each measure scores the share of the other countries beaten, using yearly data only, and parts are equally weighted. In November 2026 Ghana ranked 7 of 9 on the latest figures and 9 of 9 on 2020 to 2024 averages. The author chose this group, and another group could change the result.

Known limits

  • Equal weighting of parts and the choice of measures are judgements.
  • Sector links come from an estimated input-output table, not an official Ghana table.
  • The World Bank industry series includes manufacturing, so manufacturing partly counts twice in sector output.
  • World Bank unemployment is an ILO modelled estimate and is lower than Ghana Statistical Service's figure. It is compared only with its own series.
  • A rebased GDP or revised series can shift ratios between years.
  • Galamsey damage, regional gaps, poverty, informal work and recent tourism are not measured.
  • Ranking uses the full history for past years, so a past year's score can change slightly as new years are added.

Independent check

The November 2026 score was recalculated in a separate script and matched the page, at 53.21. This checks the arithmetic, not whether the method is the right one. It has not been reviewed by an outside economist.
